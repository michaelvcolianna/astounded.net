Before you choose

These are some things to consider when deciding on an author site.

Cost

Your main cost in all cases is likely to be the domain name. However, if you don't care about something like mysite.wordpress.com you don't even need a domain name. Other money concerns:

  • Hosting. This can be a hassle but it's where your site is stored. In some cases this is free. In other cases, it's free, but a lot of features are locked away. In yet others, there's no free plan.
  • Many monthly prices only apply if you pay for one year or more up front. The prices you see on the page take a yearly total and divide that by the number of months – meaning that you go to sign up and it wants $230.
  • A lot of places have lower prices and "included" options only for the first year.
  • If you're looking to have paid subscribers or use a platform with an ecommerce feature, that platform is probably gonna keep a percentage of any money you get. (And there may be additional transaction fees by the payment processor.)

Owning your content

Many services provide a way to export your information and take it with you elsewhere. Not all provide a comprehensive backup. Some have no backups at all. Some considerations that may not come to mind:

  • Your newsletter subscribers
  • Comments on blog posts
  • Shop sales information

How much work you want to do

The options all have varying levels of difficulty when it comes to getting started, configuring, and maintaining your website. Though there are three paths for a website, I'm going to narrow it down to two for easier comparison:

Hosted platforms
Places like WordPress.com and Squarespace. You create an account, manage your site through their dashboard, and don't have to worry beyond that for the most part.
Self-hosted
You can run something like WordPress or Ghost on a server. Or, you have a bunch of HTML files (written by hand or built statically). In either case, you have to worry about updates/configuration/other things.

What authors usually need

A page for each book (maybe a cover image, teaser text, etc.) Aside from that, you probably need links to retailers like Bookshop or Itch. You probably want a newsletter signup form (and all the "fun" that goes along with it).

If you do a lot of events, you may also want a calendar.

Speed and reliability

One of the most frequent complains I hear about the hosted platforms and self-hosted WordPress is about how slow the pages load. There's no main cause. It could be out of date software, a hack, the website's theme, or something else.

The key thing to remember is that with the hosted platforms, your ability to fix those problems is limited (if available at all). On the flip side, if you self-host, you may find fixing those problems to be a daunting task.

While not the easiest option to implement, the "bunch of HTML files" method is always going to be the fastest and most secure of all options.
